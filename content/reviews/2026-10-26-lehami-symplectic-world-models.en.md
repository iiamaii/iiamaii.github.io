LeHami replaces the predictor in a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) with a symplectic flow that preserves latent phase-space volume throughout a rollout. At the actuated-pendulum anchor, a 25-nearest-neighbor (25-NN) probe reads angular velocity with mean absolute error (MAE) of 0.419 rad/s, versus LeWorldModel (LeWM)'s 2.508 rad/s, although LeWM has lower angle error in the same table. [Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15267v1#S3.T1) **Review interpretation.** This is a convincing case for geometric constraints on latent prediction: benefits appear in velocity representation and some long-horizon predictions, while goal-reaching remains weaker than a baseline with a stronger temporal encoder.

This part follows §§1–4; [\[2/2\] proofs, design choices, and ablations](/en/reviews/lehami-symplectic-world-models-technical/) covers Appendices B–E.

## 1. Introduction

JEPA predicts representations of future observations rather than reconstructing pixels. Distributional regularization or a pretrained encoder can discourage **representation collapse**, where different observations receive the same code. A separate problem remains: even distinct encoded states can move closer as predictions feed back into the predictor. The paper calls this **predictor contraction**. Small per-step contraction accumulates during autoregressive rollout. [§1, pp.1–3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.15267v1#page=1)

The proposed remedy comes from Hamiltonian mechanics. A latent state has a position-like block $\mathbf q$ and a momentum-like block $\mathbf p$; derivatives of a scalar $H_\phi$ move the pair forward. Symplectic integration preserves the oriented area structure and volume of phase space. It allows a cloud to stretch or shear while forbidding a reduction of its total volume.

[![LeHami latent phase space, action conditioning, and three structural properties.](/assets/reviews/lehami/lehami-fig1-concept.webp)](/assets/reviews/lehami/lehami-fig1-concept.webp)

Original Figure 1. The left connects torque-conditioned observations to latent trajectories; the right illustrates volume preservation, integration between observation frames, and backward integration. Contours refer to $H_\phi$ at a fixed action. [Fig.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15267v1#S1.F1)

The authors identify three contributions: an end-to-end pixel-based model whose individual transitions preserve volume; a predictor whose integration interval can change, including sign reversal; and an explicit momentum-like channel for motion information and velocity-conditioned goals. Their two pendulum environments differ in energy conservation but both preserve phase-space volume, separating energy conservation from symplecticity. [§1 Contributions, §3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.15267v1#page=3)

## 2. LeHami: Symplectic Hamiltonian JEPA

**Proposition 1** states that a leapfrog step generated by a separable Hamiltonian $H_\phi=K_{\phi_1}(\mathbf p)+V_{\phi_2}(\mathbf q,\mathbf u)$, with action $\mathbf u$ fixed within the step, is a symplectic diffeomorphism. Appendix C.1 specifies the required $C^2$ regularity. This rules out uniform contraction in all directions, asymptotically stable fixed points, and finite-step merging of distinct initial latent states. Composing steps with different fixed actions retains these properties. [§2, Proposition 1; C.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.15267v1#page=3)

The authors immediately delimit the guarantee: error can grow despite volume preservation; distributional regularization remains necessary; and the structure is an inductive bias on latent dynamics, without identifying $H_\phi$ with physical energy. The proof and concrete examples of these distinctions are in [part 2](/en/reviews/lehami-symplectic-world-models-technical/).

### 2.1 The architecture

Training uses unannotated observation sequences $\mathbf x_t$ and actions $\mathbf u_t$. An encoder produces $\mathbf q_t$, and a fixed Savitzky–Golay (SG) differentiation stencil estimates $\mathbf p_t$ from the encoded trajectory. The experiments use 64 latent coordinates in total, with $d=32$ coordinates per block. SG adds no trainable parameters. At initialization it reads the current and three preceding latent positions; thereafter $\mathbf p$ evolves as part of the predicted state. [Eq.1, D.1–D.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.15267v1#page=4)

[![Encoder and SG initialize the state, and repeated Hamiltonian steps learn future representations.](/assets/reviews/lehami/lehami-fig2-training.webp)](/assets/reviews/lehami/lehami-fig2-training.webp)

Original Figure 2. $C$ compares predictions with representations of actual future frames; $R$ regularizes embeddings of actual observations. The anchor stencil and future training-target stencil use different windows. [Fig.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15267v1#S2.F2)

The Hamiltonian uses predictor parameters $\phi$ and a learned scale parameter $\boldsymbol\sigma\in\mathbb R^d$:

$$
H_\phi(\mathbf q,\mathbf p,\mathbf u)
=K_\phi(\mathbf S^{-1}\mathbf p)+V_\phi(\mathbf q,\mathbf u),
\qquad \mathbf S=\operatorname{diag}(e^{\boldsymbol\sigma}).
$$

The kinetic term depends only on momentum; the potential depends on position and action. Each is a three-layer, width-1024 $\tanh$ multilayer perceptron (MLP). Applying leapfrog to Hamilton's equations, $\dot{\mathbf q}=\partial H_\phi/\partial\mathbf p$ and $\dot{\mathbf p}=-\partial H_\phi/\partial\mathbf q$, gives Eq.4 over observation interval $\Delta t$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\tilde{\mathbf p}_{t+\frac12}
  &=\mathbf p_t-\frac{\Delta t}{2}\nabla_{\mathbf q}V_\phi(\mathbf q_t,\mathbf u_t),\\
\tilde{\mathbf q}_{t+1}
  &=\mathbf q_t+\Delta t\,\mathbf S^{-1}
    \nabla K_\phi(\mathbf S^{-1}\tilde{\mathbf p}_{t+\frac12}),\\
\tilde{\mathbf p}_{t+1}
  &=\tilde{\mathbf p}_{t+\frac12}
    -\frac{\Delta t}{2}\nabla_{\mathbf q}V_\phi(\tilde{\mathbf q}_{t+1},\mathbf u_t).
\end{aligned}
$$

The sequence is a half momentum kick, a full position drift, and another half kick. The extra $\mathbf S^{-1}$ in the drift follows from the chain rule. Conditioning the potential on actions makes the force a position gradient at each fixed action and permits an explicit leapfrog update. [Eqs.2–4, D.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.15267v1#page=4)

Equation 5 matches $K$ self-fed predictions after anchor frame $A$ to embeddings of the corresponding observations:

$$
\mathcal L=
\frac1K\sum_{j=1}^{K}\|\tilde{\mathbf q}_{A+j}-\mathbf q_{A+j}\|^2
+\frac{\lambda_p}{K}\sum_{j=1}^{K}\|\tilde{\mathbf p}_{A+j}-\mathbf p_{A+j}\|^2
+\lambda_{\mathcal S}\mathcal S.
$$

The experiments use $A=3$, $K=8$, $\lambda_p=1$, and $\lambda_{\mathcal S}=0.25$; squared errors are means over coordinates. Gradients pass through the encoder and the entire rollout. The Sketched-Isotropic-Gaussian Regularizer (SIGReg) acts on encoded observations, never predictions. Unless stated otherwise, LeHami regularizes $\mathbf q$ only. Part 2 explains joint and split regularization through Table 8. [Eq.5, D.1, C.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.15267v1#page=5)

### 2.2 Related work

The authors compare several Hamiltonian world-model approaches. *Physically Native World Models* is a position paper proposing observation decoding and planning. *HaM-World* separates canonical and context subspaces with an external control term. *PH-Dreamer* adds port-Hamiltonian structure to a decoder-based Dreamer transition. The closest, *H-JEPA*, uses a fixed slice of a wider perceptual code as its control state, together with dissipation, an orthonormal input port, and port-inverse consistency to read executed actions. LeHami instead places actions inside $V_\phi(\mathbf q,\mathbf u)$ and advances the entire $(\mathbf q,\mathbf p)$ state symplectically. [§2.2, pp.5–6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.15267v1#page=5)

### Review supplement: differences in the primary references

[LeWM §3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19312v1#S3.SS1) combines a per-frame encoder, a transformer reading a history of representations, and SIGReg. LeHami's pendulum comparison applies a common multi-step training setup to this framework; its comparison models differ in scale and training details from the original LeWM paper. In [H-JEPA §§3.4–3.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.33497v1#S3.SS4), a velocity difference conditions the transition, only the control state $s$ is advanced, and discretization uses explicit Euler. Sharing the Hamiltonian name does not imply the same discrete transition or action pathway.

## 3. Empirical validation of LeHami

### 3.1 Experimental setup

Both environments render $64\times64$ RGB observations of pendulum angle $\theta$ and angular velocity $\omega$. The conservative pendulum has three lengths without actuation. The actuated pendulum has three lengths and three masses, with recorded torques. Episodes have 80 frames at 0.1 s spacing. Train/validation/test splits contain 210/45/45 conservative episodes and 630/135/135 actuated episodes, with entire episodes assigned to splits. [§3.1; Table 3, D.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.15267v1#page=23)

The main LeWorldModel (LeWM) comparator uses a causal transformer over three 64-dimensional latent embeddings. Data, training schedule, and encoder are matched; LeHami changes the predictor and adds SG. The default pixel encoder is a per-frame ConvNet. Baselines also include $W=3$ temporal encoders with tied or untied weights; the experimental $W$ denotes the **number of input frames**. Reward-dependent Dreamer or TD-MPC agents are outside this reward-free goal-conditioned comparison. [§3.1, D.1–D.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.15267v1#page=6)

Three probe tiers read representations: ridge regression, 25-NN, and a two-layer MLP. Angle probes predict $\sin\theta,\cos\theta$ and recover the angle with $\operatorname{atan2}$; angular differences are wrapped to $[-\pi,\pi)$ before MAE. Velocity MAE is in rad/s. Lower is better for both MAEs; $R^2(\omega)=1-\mathrm{SSE}/\mathrm{SST}$ is higher-is-better, with negative values worse than predicting the mean. SSE sums squared prediction residuals; SST sums squared target deviations from their mean. For rollout comparisons, 25-NN is fitted once at each model's $K=1$ output and frozen, giving LeWM's predictor an opportunity to process its historical context. [§3.1, D.4.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.15267v1#page=22)

On the conservative pendulum, LeHami has better velocity readout from the start; angle errors are initially close and favor LeHami from approximately $K=11$. The authors report about 2.8 times lower velocity MAE over $K=1$–10. The interval table and the latent blocks' own errors are examined together in [part 2, E.2](/en/reviews/lehami-symplectic-world-models-technical/). ### 3.3 LeHami is a world model with time re-stepping and reversal

For the conservative pendulum, the authors fix learned $H_\phi$ and change only the integration interval. Training uses $\Delta t=0.1$ s. Integrating twice or four times per frame, at 0.05 or 0.025 s, produces similar angle-MAE curves. The target is a Runge–Kutta integration of the actual pendulum ODE from the same initial conditions. Doubling the interval to 0.2 s instead sharply increases error: symplectic structure and numerical stability are separate requirements. [§3.3, Fig.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15267v1#S3.F5)

[![Prediction error at unseen step sizes and round-trip error when the integration interval changes sign.](/assets/reviews/lehami/lehami-fig5-time.webp)](/assets/reviews/lehami/lehami-fig5-time.webp)

Original Figure 5. Left: angle MAE on a logarithmic axis against physical time integrated. Right: relative latent round-trip error (%) after $K$ forward steps followed by steps with negative $\Delta t$. It remains below $10^{-4}\%$ over the displayed $K\le30$. This inverse-integration diagnostic measures something different from agreement with the physical ground-truth trajectory.

### 3.4 LeHami brings computational efficiency and structural priors in velocity planning

Planning is evaluated only on the actuated pendulum. The same 50 test goals are recorded states 30 frames after their corresponding starts, making the goals reachable. They are not restricted to upright rest. Each model encodes its own start and goal; the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) searches action sequences minimizing terminal latent distance:

$$
\mathcal C(\tilde{\mathbf z}_H)=\|\tilde{\mathbf z}_H-\mathbf z_g\|^2.
$$

For LeHami, $\mathbf z=(\mathbf q,\mathbf p)$. Physical coordinates and probe outputs do not enter this cost. CEM uses 300 candidates, 30 iterations, and 30 elites. Torques satisfy $|u|\le4$ and are held for three frames. Model Predictive Control (MPC) executes the first $R$ steps, observes the actual system, and replans over the time remaining until the same goal instant. Success is scored **at that instant**, separately and jointly for angle error $<0.1$ rad and velocity error $<0.5$ rad/s. [§3.4; D.4.2, pp.23–24](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.15267v1#page=23)

Table 2 selects the **best success rate** for each clause from $R\in\{30,15,9,6,3\}$ configurations costing at most 15 TFLOP/episode. It uses three model seeds, each with three planner seeds and 50 goals. Thus a reported average is not a success count from one 50-trial run: 0.800 averages rates based on 150 trials per model seed.

| Model | Position success | Velocity success | Joint success |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| LeWM $W=1$ | **0.800±0.019** | 0.184±0.027 | 0.142±0.033 |
| LeWM $W=3$, tied | 0.633±0.020 | 0.473±0.014 | 0.400±0.037 |
| LeWM $W=3$, untied | 0.696±0.016 | **0.800±0.058** | **0.636±0.025** |

LeHami uses $R=9$ at 8.05 TFLOP for position and $R=6$ at 10.98 TFLOP for velocity and joint success. Untied LeWM uses $R=9$, 13.65 TFLOP throughout. LeWM $W=1$ uses $R=9$, 12.87 TFLOP for position/joint and $R=15$, 8.78 TFLOP for velocity. Tied LeWM uses $R=9$, 13.65 TFLOP for position/velocity and $R=15$, 9.31 TFLOP for joint success. [Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15267v1#S3.T2)

Pooling the raw velocity-clause trials gives 237 successes out of 450 for LeHami, 213 for tied LeWM, and 360 for untied LeWM. These repeat the same 50 goals across three model seeds and three planner seeds.

Tied temporal weights can reveal speed while discarding direction. Untying them yields the strongest velocity and joint results. LeHami's cheaper flow permits more frequent replanning for the velocity clause, but positional goal-reaching limits its joint success. Part 2 examines direction-specific success, the goal distribution, and parameter and arithmetic costs. [§3.4, E.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.15267v1#page=9)

## 4. Conclusion

The authors conclude that a symplectic transition in a self-supervised latent space can structurally exclude specific contraction failures at evaluation. Conservative-pendulum rollouts, sub-frame integration, and inverse integration illustrate its use. They also acknowledge LeWM's strength on positional goals and the importance of breaking temporal symmetry to capture velocity in the baseline. [§4, p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.15267v1#page=9)

Their proposed extensions include port-Hamiltonian modeling of strong dissipation such as friction, choosing structures according to static-pose or velocity-aware tasks, and learning momentum embeddings instead of fixing SG. The experiments here cover two volume-preserving pendulum environments.

## Review interpretation and open questions

**Review interpretation.** The most useful conceptual contribution separates a constraint on the training distribution from one on every inference transition. It answers a repeated-prediction failure with a mathematical property of each step. The large anchor-velocity gap also reflects an information difference: LeHami's SG reads changes across four instants, whereas the per-frame LeWM goal uses one. The untied temporal baseline therefore provides an especially consequential comparison.

**Review interpretation.** A useful next empirical test would give a strong baseline the same temporal information and goal cost, isolating the extra contribution of geometric constraints over long horizons. Here the predictor, SG pathway, and goal representation change together, so the gain is not fully decomposed by component. The appendix's regularization failures, latent errors, and cost measurements provide necessary evidence for that question and are examined in [part 2](/en/reviews/lehami-symplectic-world-models-technical/).
